Choosing a Water Damage Restoration Company in Fairhaven, MA

The questions worth asking, the red flags to watch for and how to compare companies when you are stressed and in a hurry.

☎ Call (774) 483-4896

By the Fairhaven Water Damage Pros team

Water damage rarely happens at a convenient time. You are tired, the floor is wet and someone offers to start right now. That is exactly when it pays to know what a good company looks like.

This guide gives you a short list of questions, signs of a reliable scope and warning signs. It does not name companies, and it applies to anyone you consider, including us.

Ask how they will measure drying

Good drying is measured. Ask what meters they use, how often they will take readings and what numbers mean the job is done. A company that dries by guesswork may leave moisture in walls.

Ask to see readings from unaffected areas used as a dry standard. This is how crews know when materials match normal conditions.

Look for a clear written scope

The scope should list rooms, materials, equipment and the work planned. It should explain how price is set and what could change it. Vague or verbal promises are a risk.

Extraction, demolition, drying and monitoring should each appear as their own line, with the rooms they apply to.

Ask about insurance and credentials

Ask whether the company carries liability insurance and whether the trades involved have the credentials Massachusetts requires. Ask for documents, not just a verbal yes. Ask whether the crew follows published standards such as ANSI/IICRC S500 for water and S520 for mold.

Standards do not guarantee quality, but a crew that knows them can explain its choices.

Watch for pressure and shortcuts

Be careful of anyone who pressures you to sign an assignment of benefits or contract on the spot, or who tells you that insurance will cover everything. Your insurer decides coverage. Read documents before you sign them.

Also be careful of anyone who wants to throw away everything without explaining why.

Compare at least two when you can

In a true emergency you may have time for only one call. Even then, ask the questions above. For larger jobs, get a second opinion on the scope.

Questions about the crew

Ask who will be in your home, how many people and whether they are employees or subcontractors. Ask who the lead is and how to reach them. A good company can answer this in a sentence.

Ask also how they protect floors and belongings during work, how they handle debris and how they leave the site each day.

Why written documentation matters

Photographs, moisture logs and equipment records are the proof that work was done. They help your insurer, and they help you if a problem shows up months later. Ask what documents you will receive and when.

A company that shows its records readily is usually one that keeps them.

  • Photos before, during and after.
  • Moisture readings each visit.
  • Equipment placed and removed dates.
  • A final summary of work done.

What to do if something goes wrong

If you feel rushed or uncomfortable, stop and ask questions. You can call the company again, ask for the scope in writing and get another opinion. A reputable company will understand.

If you believe a contractor acted unfairly, the Massachusetts Attorney General's consumer resources can explain your options. Keep every document you signed.

Timing: what to do while you decide

You do not have to choose a company before the first protective steps. Close the main shutoff, lift valuables, photograph the damage and take notes on how the water got in. Those steps take no contractor and cost nothing.

Once you call, ask for the first visit to focus on extraction and moisture mapping. Decisions about demolition and rebuild can wait until readings are in and you have seen a written scope.

Red flags that deserve a second thought

Be careful when a company refuses to give a written scope, will not say how drying is measured, asks for full payment up front or tells you not to call your insurer. Be careful of door-to-door offers after a storm from people you cannot verify.

A reliable company is comfortable with questions. If you feel brushed off, call someone else.

Choosing in the Fairhaven area

Local knowledge matters. A company that knows Fairhaven's older homes, the coastal storm history recorded in state records, and the Building Department's process can tell you what to expect. Ask where the crew is based and who answers the phone.

Ask about service in New Bedford and Acushnet if you live outside Fairhaven.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I call my insurer first?

Many people call both. Your insurer decides coverage, and the restoration company starts protecting the property.

Is the cheapest quote the best?

Not necessarily. Compare what is included, how drying is measured and what could change the price.

Do I have to use my insurer's preferred company?

Check your policy and ask your insurer. You often have a choice.

What if I already signed something?

Read it again and ask questions. You may have rights to cancel in some cases, and an attorney can advise.

Should I get more than one quote after a flood?

If you can, yes. If water is still spreading, start protection first and compare scopes as time allows.
