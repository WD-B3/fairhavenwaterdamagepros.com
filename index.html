Before we arrive

What to do while water is spreading

These steps follow general guidance for water losses, including the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard, and apply only if you can do them safely.

  • Close the main water shutoff or the valve at the leaking fixture.
  • Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the switch while dry.
  • Keep people and pets out of water that touches outlets or appliances.
  • Lift furniture, papers and electronics off wet floors.
  • Photograph the source and the damage before cleanup.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.
Fairhaven

Water damage on Buzzards Bay

Fairhaven sits at the mouth of the Acushnet River, across from New Bedford, with Buzzards Bay on its southern edge. That position shapes the water damage we see. Storms push tide and wind-driven rain at the coast, and old plumbing and old cellars add problems from the inside.

Fort Phoenix State Reservation, at the mouth of the river, lies seaward of the New Bedford Harbor Hurricane Barrier, a reminder that parts of town take storms without that protection. The town's climate vulnerability assessment says the same of Sconticut Neck, West Island and Shaw's Cove, and that Goulart Memorial Drive flooded on December 23, 2022.

The town's hazard mitigation plan counts about 7,447 housing units, with nearly 3,000 built before 1940. Older housing is common across Fairhaven, and a burst supply line or a damp wall is a familiar story in Fairhaven Center, East Fairhaven, North Fairhaven, Poverty Point, Nasketucket and Winsegansett Heights.

Commercial water loss follows the same map. Route 6, called Huttleston Avenue in town, carries much of the commercial traffic, and the assessment anticipates fringe flooding at the waterfront, such as Union Wharf. Read the flood damage cleanup page and the commercial water damage page to see how those losses are handled.

Documenting your loss for insurance

Your insurer decides coverage, and policies differ. We take photos, record moisture readings and keep equipment logs so you have a clear file to share. Standard homeowners policies often exclude flood and sewer backup unless added, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, so read yours early.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do first when water is spreading in my Fairhaven home?

Stop the source if you can, usually by closing the main shutoff. Keep people and pets out of water that touches outlets or appliances, move valuables up and photograph the damage. Then call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Do you answer calls at night and on weekends?

Yes. The line is open 24/7, including holidays and storm nights. Dispatch arranges the earliest response it can once it knows the address and the problem.

Will my homeowners insurance cover water damage?

That depends on your policy and the cause. Sudden events such as a burst pipe are often covered, while flood and sewer backup usually need separate coverage, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance. Photos, moisture logs and receipts help whatever your insurer decides.

How do you know when a house is dry?

Crews take moisture readings in affected materials and compare them with readings from unaffected areas. The job is finished when the numbers match, not when surfaces feel dry. See the structural drying page for detail.

Which towns do you serve besides Fairhaven?

New Bedford, Mattapoisett, Acushnet, North Dartmouth and Marion each have a page on this site. Call and describe where you are if your town is not listed.

Is there a form I can fill out instead of calling?

No. Water damage is time sensitive, so this site is call-only. Dial the number on any page and speak to dispatch.
