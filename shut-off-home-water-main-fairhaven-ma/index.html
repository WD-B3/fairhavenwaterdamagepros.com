How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Fairhaven, MA

Where the valve usually is, how to test it before you need it and what to do after you close it.

☎ Call (774) 483-4896

By the Fairhaven Water Damage Pros team

The first thing a plumber or restoration crew asks during a leak is whether you have turned off the water. If you do not know where the main shutoff is, the answer is expensive. Finding it takes ten minutes on a calm day.

This guide explains where to look, how to test the valve, what the street valve is and what to do after the water is off.

Where the main shutoff usually is

In most homes the main shutoff is where the water line enters the house, often in the basement near the front wall, in a utility room or in a crawl space. Look near the water meter. Houses on slabs may have it in a utility closet or outside.

Mark it with a tag so anyone in the house can find it fast.

Gate valves and ball valves

An older gate valve has a round wheel handle that turns clockwise to close. A newer ball valve has a lever handle that turns a quarter turn. Gate valves can stick or break if forced, so test them gently.

If the valve does not close fully, have a plumber replace it before you need it.

Test it now, not later

With the house calm, close the main, open a faucet to confirm the flow stops and then reopen the valve slowly. If it leaks around the handle or will not turn, call a plumber.

Also find the shutoffs under sinks and behind toilets, which can stop a small leak without cutting off the whole house.

After you shut it off

Open a low faucet to relieve pressure. If a pipe burst, turn off the water heater to protect it. Photograph the water and damage, move valuables and call a plumber to repair the line.

If water has reached floors or walls, the burst pipe page explains what comes next, and the extraction page covers removal.

The street valve belongs to the utility

A valve at the curb controls the service line to your house. It usually belongs to the utility, so do not try to operate it without permission. Call the water department if you cannot reach the main inside.

Know who to call after hours, and keep that number with your insurance papers.

Fixture shutoffs and appliance valves

Many leaks can be stopped without shutting off the whole house. Toilets have a small valve behind the bowl, sinks have two below the basin, and washing machines have a pair of hot and cold valves behind the machine. Learn where these are in your home.

Turn them every year so they do not seize. If a valve drips when you close it or will not turn, replace it before a failure.

Teaching the household

Everyone who lives in the house should know where the main shutoff is and how to close it. Show older children and any guests staying a while. Put a tag on the valve and a note on the refrigerator.

In an emergency, the person at home may not be the one who knows the house best. A tag and a note remove the guesswork.

  • Tag the main valve.
  • Post the plumber and dispatch numbers.
  • Practice once a year.
  • Replace stiff valves.

Draining the system and restarting

After you close the main, open the lowest faucet and the highest faucet to let the system drain. This relieves pressure and drains water from the lines. Turn off the water heater if the line will stay closed for long.

When the repair is complete, open the main slowly and check for leaks before you restore power to the heater. Run each faucet until air clears.

Leak sensors and automatic shutoff valves

Plug-in or battery sensors can send an alert if water reaches the floor. Some systems pair with a motorized valve that closes the main automatically. They cost more, but they shorten how long water runs when you are away.

A plumber can install a valve on the main. Sensors you place yourself under sinks, near the water heater and by the washer.

Shutoff tips for older houses

Older homes may have valves that have not moved in decades, or shutoffs buried behind finished walls. Find yours before you need it, and if it is hard to reach, ask a plumber to move or replace it. Mark any access panel so it does not get covered.

Some older houses have more than one entry point for water, such as a separate line for an outbuilding or irrigation. Know them all.

Code and standards for shutoff valves

The Massachusetts Plumbing Code (248 CMR) governs main shutoff valve work, so ask a plumber to replace a bad valve. Replacement is not a do-it-yourself job when the line is under pressure.

Fairhaven water service

The town's Board of Public Works handles utilities, and water comes through the Mattapoisett River Valley Water District, per its 2023 water quality report. Call the Fairhaven Water Department if you cannot find your meter or main.

Homes in Acushnet and North Dartmouth may have different utilities, so check where yours is.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I shut off the water when I leave for vacation?

Many people do, especially in winter. It cuts the risk of a long-running leak.

What if my main valve is stuck?

Do not force it. Call a plumber, and call the water department if you need the street valve.

Does shutting the main stop the water heater from leaking?

It stops new water entering, but the tank may still hold water. Shut off the heater and consider draining it.

Who do I call in Fairhaven for a water emergency?

Call the 24/7 line for restoration, and a plumber for repairs.

Should I turn off the main when I go to bed?

Most people do not, but some owners of seasonal homes do. A leak sensor is a lighter alternative.
