When water is spreading in Mattapoisett

Close the main shutoff first, then keep people away from any water that touches electrical equipment. Photograph the damage before moving anything. A leak in a vacant home may have been running for a while, so tell the crew when you last saw the house dry.

Call the 24/7 line and crews will explain extraction and drying. See the burst pipe page for what that looks like.

Coastal storms and wind-driven rain

Homes close to the bay take wind and spray that drive water through windows, siding and roof edges. Hurricane Bob in 1991 brought hurricane-force winds across southeast Massachusetts, according to state records.

For water that came in through the building envelope, see the storm water damage page. Rising water is a flood loss, covered on the flood page.

Second homes and vacant properties

A house that sits empty is a house nobody checks. Know where your main shutoff is, consider having someone look in after storms and keep your insurer's number handy. Water that runs unnoticed soaks framing before it shows.

If you return to a wet house, the structural drying page explains how readings guide the work.

Paperwork across the county line

Because Mattapoisett is in Plymouth County, permits and records go to local town offices, not Bristol County ones. Your insurer will want photos, moisture logs and receipts either way.

Ask the town building department whether your repairs need a permit before work begins.

Paperwork and planning for a seasonal property

If you own a second home, write down the name of someone who can let the crew in, and the location of the water shutoff. Give that person the dispatch number.

Keep a photo record of the interior from your last visit. It shows what changed if a leak happens while you are away.

Before the crew arrives in Mattapoisett

Work through these steps if it is safe, and call the line as soon as you can.

Shut off the water

Close the main valve and open a low faucet to relieve pressure.

Keep power safe

Switch off circuits to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and reachable.

Protect belongings

Raise furniture, rugs and papers, and keep pets out of the water.

Call the line

Call the 24/7 line and note the time you found the water.
