This page covers water that entered through the building envelope during a storm, such as wind-driven rain through windows, damaged shingles or siding. Rising water from outside is covered on the flood page, and a simple roof leak is on the ceiling page.

Storms that shape Fairhaven's risk

Fairhaven sits on Buzzards Bay at the mouth of the Acushnet River. The state's history of 20th century hurricanes records Bob in 1991 with hurricane-force winds across most of southeast Massachusetts and a Buzzards Bay storm surge of 10 to 15 feet. Hurricane Carol in 1954 brought a surge over 14 feet to New Bedford.

Those storms were extreme, but nor'easters and tropical systems bring wind-driven rain more often. Wind drives water sideways into places rain normally does not reach.

Areas outside the hurricane barrier

The town's climate vulnerability assessment notes that Sconticut Neck, West Island and Shaw's Cove residential areas sit outside the New Bedford Hurricane Barrier and experience unmitigated coastal storms. A December 23, 2022 storm flooded low-lying areas outside the barrier, especially Goulart Memorial Drive, the causeway between Sconticut Neck and West Island.

If you live in those areas, check your flood zone, keep valuables above floor level and know your evacuation zone. The state publishes a Fairhaven hurricane evacuation zone map.

How water enters during a storm

Common paths include lifted shingles, failed flashing, window frames, skylights, siding and chimneys. Wind can also push water up under eaves, and clogged gutters can send runoff into walls. Water often travels along framing before it shows.

Interior signs include stains on ceilings or walls, bubbling paint and a musty smell. The ceiling water damage page covers the finished-ceiling side, and the wall and drywall page covers walls.

Cleanup, temporary protection and repairs

Crews extract water, check moisture in affected areas and dry the structure. Temporary measures, such as tarps over roof openings, protect the building until a roofer can repair it. Roofing and siding repair are separate trades.

If the loss includes rising water, flood damage rules apply and the flood damage page explains contamination and insurance differences.

Insurance after a storm

Wind-driven rain damage is often covered when the storm created an opening in the building, but flood is typically excluded from standard homeowners policies, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance. Hurricane and wind deductibles can differ from standard deductibles, so read your policy.

Photograph before and after, keep receipts for temporary repairs and report the loss promptly.

Safety checklist after a storm

Storm hazards continue after the rain stops. Downed lines, weakened ceilings and standing water that hides electrical risk are common. Follow local officials, and the FEMA flood map and the state's evacuation guidance can help you understand your zone.

Do not enter a flooded basement or a room with a bulging ceiling.

  • Stay away from downed wires and call the utility.
  • Avoid standing water near outlets.
  • Do not use generators indoors or in garages.
  • Photograph every damaged area before cleanup.
  • Call your insurer and the 24/7 line.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle storm water damage emergencies at night or on weekends in Fairhaven?

Yes. Storms arrive at any hour, and the Fairhaven line is open 24/7 including storm nights. Call once it is safe, describe the damage, and dispatch arranges the earliest response available.

Is storm water damage covered by homeowners insurance?

Often when the storm created an opening, but flood is usually excluded. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Can I tarp the roof myself?

Climbing onto a wet roof is dangerous. Call a roofer or a crew that provides temporary protection.

How long after a storm should I call?

As soon as it is safe. Water that sits can spread into insulation and framing.

What if power is out?

Crews can still dry a structure, and dispatch can advise on safe steps while power is down.
