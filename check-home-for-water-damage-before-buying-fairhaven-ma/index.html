Checking a Fairhaven, MA Home for Water Damage Before Buying

How to spot stains, smells and hidden moisture during a showing, and what questions to ask before closing on a house.

☎ Call (774) 483-4896

By the Fairhaven Water Damage Pros team

A house can look perfect on a sunny Saturday and still hide a wet basement. Buyers rarely see the first rainstorm, so they have to look for evidence of what the house has been through.

This guide shows what to check while you tour, what to ask the seller and what a home inspector can and cannot tell you.

Walk through with your nose and hands

Smell the basement and closets. A musty odor suggests moisture. Look at ceilings and walls for stains, even faded ones, and press on baseboards and floors that feel soft. Look for fresh paint in odd spots.

Check the corners where walls meet floors, and look inside cabinets under sinks.

Basements and crawl spaces

Look for water lines on the walls, efflorescence (white powder), rust on the bottom of the furnace or water heater and a sump pit with an old discharge pipe. Ask whether the pump has run recently.

A crawl space with vapor barrier gaps or wet insulation is a warning sign. The crawl space page explains why.

Ask the seller good questions

Ask if the house has ever had water in the basement, a roof leak, a burst pipe or an insurance claim. Ask for repair records. Ask what the sump pump and gutters have done in big storms.

Disclosure rules are a legal matter, so involve your attorney or agent.

The inspection and its limits

A home inspector looks at visible conditions, often with a moisture meter, and writes a report. They generally cannot see inside walls or under finished floors. If something looks suspicious, ask for a specialist, such as a moisture assessment.

The structural drying page explains how moisture is measured.

Flood zones and insurance

Check the FEMA flood map for the address. Ask your insurer about flood coverage before you close, since standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance.

If you find damage after closing, read the water extraction page for the first steps.

A buyer's quick checklist for the tour

You will not have long at a showing, so know what to look at first. Start in the basement, then the lowest bathroom, then the kitchen sink area, then the top floor ceilings below the roof. Use your phone flashlight in corners.

Take photos of anything odd so you can ask your inspector about it later.

  • Smell the basement and closets.
  • Look at ceilings and wall corners for stains.
  • Open cabinet doors under sinks.
  • Look at the foundation for cracks and white deposits.

Questions about the roof, gutters and grading

Ask how old the roof is and whether it has leaked. Look at gutters for staining or sagging. Check that soil slopes away from the foundation and that downspouts end away from the house.

Poor grading sends water to the foundation, and rooflines that dump onto walls cause repeated stains. These are fixable, but you want to know their cost before you buy.

After closing: first steps

Once you own the house, learn where the main water shutoff is and test it. Check under every sink, look at the water heater and note the age of appliances. Take dated photos of everything as a baseline.

If you find hidden damage, document it before you touch anything, and talk to your inspector and attorney about next steps.

Documents to ask for before closing

Ask the seller for any repair invoices, warranties and permits tied to water problems, along with the age of the roof, water heater and sump pump. Ask whether the basement has ever taken on water and whether insurance claims were filed.

Your attorney can request records and review them with you. If the seller has none, treat the gap as a reason to inspect more carefully, not as proof of a problem.

  • Repair invoices and warranties.
  • Permits for plumbing, electrical and structural work.
  • Age of roof, heater and pump.
  • Any flood zone determination.

Buying near Buzzards Bay

In Fairhaven, ask about the flood zone near Fort Phoenix, Sconticut Neck and West Island. The town's vulnerability assessment says residential areas outside the New Bedford Harbor Hurricane Barrier face unmitigated coastal storms, and Goulart Memorial Drive flooded on December 23, 2022.

Buyers in Mattapoisett and New Bedford should ask the same questions.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should I get a flood zone check before an offer?

Yes. The FEMA flood map shows the zone for a specific address.

Can I ask for moisture readings?

You can ask your inspector to bring a meter, and you can ask for a specialist if readings look high.

Is a stained ceiling a dealbreaker?

Not necessarily, but you want to know the cause and whether it is fixed.

Who do I call about a house I just bought?

If you find active water, call the 24/7 line and take photos for your records.

Is a musty smell a reason to walk away?

Not always, but find out why. It may be a dry fix or a larger problem. Ask your inspector for a moisture assessment.
