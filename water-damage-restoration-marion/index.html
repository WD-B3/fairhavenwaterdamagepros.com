Water damage in Marion, MA is shaped by the bay. The town sits at the head of Buzzards Bay near Sippican Harbor, and the dispatch line for Fairhaven Water Damage Pros is open 24/7 when wind, tide or a burst pipe brings water into a home.

This page begins with Marion's coastline and then explains how crews handle storm water, seasonal homes and the questions that come with a Plymouth County address.

Harbor and bay

Coastal exposure

County and permits

Coastal storms in Marion

Hurricane Bob in 1991 sent a surge of 10 to 15 feet up Buzzards Bay, according to state records of 20th century hurricanes, and the head of the bay felt it. Nor'easters and tropical systems still bring wind-driven rain and high tides.

When water is spreading in Marion

Stop the source if you can, and stay out of floodwater, which may be contaminated and may hide electrical hazards. Move belongings up, photograph the water line and call the line when it is safe.

Crews extract and dry. Water from the bay or a backed-up drain is handled as contaminated, as the sewage page describes.

Seasonal and second homes

Many coastal homes sit closed for part of the year. A burst pipe in a shuttered house can run for days. Draining the system, keeping heat on low and having someone check after storms all reduce the risk.

The frozen pipe page explains winter breaks in detail.

Crawl spaces and low foundations

Coastal houses are often built on piers, crawl spaces or low foundations where humid air and groundwater collect. Damp insulation and musty smells under floors are common signs.

The crawl space page explains moisture control, and the mold page covers growth.

Insurance for coastal properties

Flood is generally excluded from standard homeowners policies, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, and wind deductibles can differ from standard ones. Read your policy before storm season and keep your documents handy.

After a loss, record the time, take photos and keep receipts for temporary repairs.

Safety checklist for coastal homes

Floodwater and bay water can carry contaminants and hide electrical hazards. Follow state evacuation guidance if you are in a flood zone.

These steps apply once it is safe to be in the house.

  • Stay out of standing water near outlets.
  • Shut off the main water valve.
  • Photograph the water line on walls.
  • Move belongings above the waterline.
  • Call the 24/7 line.

Sippican Harbor, the shoreline and vacant homes

Waterfront homes in Marion can sit empty for months, and roofs and windows take wind and spray all year. A short check after every big storm catches small leaks before they spread.

If you return to a wet house, photograph it, shut off the water and call. The record of what you saw first helps your insurer and the crew.

Septic systems and wells

Many Marion properties use septic systems rather than sewers. A backup into the house is a sewage loss, and the crew will use protective gear and treat it as contaminated. Ask the town board of health about septic questions.

Heavy rain can saturate a leach field and slow drainage, so avoid heavy water use during and after storms.
