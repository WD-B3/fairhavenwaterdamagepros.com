Appliance and supply line leaks

Washing machines, dishwashers, refrigerator lines and water heaters cause many losses in busy households. Rubber hoses harden with age, and a failed fitting can release gallons per minute. Braided lines and an annual look at shutoffs reduce the risk.

The appliance leak page and the water heater page explain each case.

When water is spreading in North Dartmouth

Close the shutoff for the fixture or the main valve if you cannot find it. Take photos of the damage and the source before cleaning. If a neighbor's unit is the source, notify the landlord in writing.

Call the 24/7 line once you are safe. Crews arrange extraction and drying, and the water extraction page explains the equipment.

Renting here: tenants and landlords

Leases decide who repairs what, and renters insurance often covers belongings but not the building. Landlords should document the unit, tenants should keep receipts and everyone should keep written records.

The rental guide covers notices and documentation.

Ceilings, kitchens and drying afterward

Upper-floor leaks drop through ceilings into kitchens and bedrooms below. The ceiling water damage page explains why a ceiling stain needs a look above, and the structural drying page explains how readings guide drying.

Do not paint over a stain until the cause is fixed and the area is dry.

Safety checklist and standards for rentals and homes

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard guides how professionals treat water by category, and the Massachusetts State Sanitary Code sets minimum housing standards for rentals. Follow these steps while you wait.

Tell your landlord in writing if you rent.

  • Close the shutoff for the fixture or the main valve.
  • Stay off wet floors near outlets.
  • Photograph the source and damage.
  • Move belongings off wet floors.
  • Call the 24/7 line.

Student move-out season and shared houses

Rentals turn over in late spring and late summer, and empty units can hide leaks for weeks. Landlords should walk each unit after move-out and turn on every faucet. Tenants should check under sinks on move-in.

A small drip left unchecked can warp a floor before the next tenant arrives.

Streets in North Dartmouth are a short drive from Fairhaven, and many people cross the line for work, school and shopping. If you live near the border, tell dispatch your exact address so the crew goes the right way.
