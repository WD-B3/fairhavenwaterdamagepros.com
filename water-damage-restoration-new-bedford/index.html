New Bedford at a glance for water damage

Where we cover

All six New Bedford ZIP codes, 02740, 02741, 02742, 02744, 02745 and 02746, and emergency dispatch available around the clock.

Waterfront setting

The city sits on the west bank of the Acushnet River at the head of New Bedford Harbor, which opens into Buzzards Bay.

Hurricane barrier

The New Bedford Harbor Hurricane Barrier protects the inner harbor from storm surge, though it does not stop rain or groundwater.

County and permits

New Bedford is in Bristol County. Repair permits go through the city, so ask the New Bedford building inspector what applies to your job.

When water is spreading in New Bedford

Start by stopping the source. If a pipe has burst, close the main shutoff. If water is coming through a ceiling, keep clear of the area and call. Standing water near outlets or the panel is a reason to call your utility or 911 about electrical risk before dispatch.

Once it is safe, the 24/7 line sends crews who extract water and set drying equipment. The water extraction page explains what that visit involves.

Harbor flooding and the barrier

State records of 20th century hurricanes say Hurricane Carol in 1954 brought a storm surge of more than 14 feet to New Bedford, and Hurricane Bob in 1991 pushed a surge of 10 to 15 feet up Buzzards Bay. The hurricane barrier was built afterward to shield the inner harbor.

Low areas near the waterfront can still see tidal and storm flooding. Check the FEMA flood map for your address, and read the flood damage cleanup page before storm season.

Older multi-unit buildings

Much of New Bedford's housing is older, and many buildings hold several apartments stacked above one another. A leak in an upper unit can run through ceilings and walls below, so the first call is often about a neighbor's water, not your own.

Tenants should photograph damage and notify the landlord in writing. The rental guide explains who handles what.

Commercial and mixed-use properties

The working waterfront, shops and offices around downtown have different needs than a house. Business owners need fast containment, clear documentation and a plan for stock and records.

Read the commercial water damage page for how crews limit downtime.

Insurance questions we hear from New Bedford callers

Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, and many exclude sewer backup unless it was added, according to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance. Landlords and tenants carry different coverage, so read your own policy.

Keep photos, moisture logs and receipts, and ask your insurer before you discard damaged items.

If water is still spreading in New Bedford

Take these steps while you wait for the crew, and only if it is safe to do so.

Stop the source

Close the main shutoff or the valve at the fixture that is leaking.

Power down safely

Turn off power to wet areas only if you can reach the switch while dry.

Lift valuables

Move furniture legs, papers and electronics off wet floors.

Call dispatch

Call the 24/7 line and describe the water, the source and how far it has spread.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you serve both sides of the harbor?

Yes. Fairhaven and New Bedford face each other across the Acushnet River, and crews serve both.

Will the hurricane barrier keep my basement dry?

No. The barrier controls storm surge in the harbor, but rain, groundwater and plumbing failures can still flood a basement.

What if the leak is in the apartment above mine?

Photograph your damage, notify your landlord in writing and call. Crews can document and dry your unit.

Do you cover every ZIP code in New Bedford?

Yes. Coverage includes 02740, 02741, 02742, 02744, 02745 and 02746, from the harbor waterfront to the neighborhoods west of Route 18. The primary ZIP for this page is 02740.

Is emergency water removal available in New Bedford?

Yes. Night, weekend or storm, the line is staffed. Tell dispatch what is wet and where it came from, and the earliest visit possible is arranged for your New Bedford address.
