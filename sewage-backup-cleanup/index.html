Sewage is the most contaminated kind of water loss. This page explains why it is handled differently from a burst pipe, what crews wear and remove, and how the loss is cleaned and dried.

Sewage coming up a drain: what it means

Searchers often describe it in plain words. Water gurgles in a tub, a toilet will not flush, a basement floor drain bubbles or dark water rises in a shower. These point to a blocked or overloaded sewer line, and they mean wastewater is flowing the wrong way into your home.

Backups are Category 3 water under the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, meaning grossly contaminated. That category sets the rules for protective gear, containment and which materials must be discarded.

Why sewage cannot be treated like clean water

Sewage carries bacteria, viruses and parasites. Porous materials such as carpet, pad, upholstered furniture and drywall that were soaked are usually removed rather than cleaned. Hard surfaces can be cleaned and disinfected.

Health questions belong to a physician. If anyone has been exposed and feels ill, contact a doctor and tell them what happened.

Who is responsible for the blockage

A blockage inside your house, such as a clogged branch line, is a plumber's job. A blockage in the street main is a municipal matter. Fairhaven's Board of Public Works handles town utilities, and its wastewater treatment plant sits on Arsene Street.

The town's climate vulnerability assessment emphasizes the risk coastal flooding poses to town pump stations, so heavy storms can overload sewer systems. If you suspect a municipal issue, report it to the town and keep photos.

How cleanup works

Crews wear protective gear, seal off clean areas and extract contaminated water. Affected porous materials are removed and bagged. Surfaces are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products, then dried with air movers and dehumidifiers.

Moisture readings and documentation follow, and a plumber confirms the line is clear before rebuilding. For pumping details see water extraction, and for tidal flooding see flood damage cleanup.

Insurance and sewer backup coverage

Standard residential policies exclude sewer backup, the Massachusetts Division of Insurance explains, though most insurers sell an endorsement for it. Some of those endorsements still leave out backup caused by flood, so find out exactly what yours says.

Take photos, list discarded items and keep receipts. Do not throw away contaminated belongings until your insurer has had a chance to document them, unless they are a safety risk.

Reporting and documenting

Write down the time you noticed the backup, which fixtures were affected and whether neighbors have the same problem. Photographs of the water line on walls help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you handle sewage backup cleanup emergencies at night or on weekends in Fairhaven?

Yes. Backups happen at night and on weekends, and the Fairhaven line is open 24/7. Call from a safe distance, describe what is coming up, and dispatch arranges the earliest response it can.

Can I clean up sewage myself?

Small amounts on hard surfaces can sometimes be cleaned with proper gloves and disinfectant, but larger spills or anything soaked into porous materials should be handled by trained crews.

Why is carpet thrown away after a backup?

Porous materials soaked in sewage cannot be reliably disinfected, so they are usually removed.

How do I know if the problem is the town's sewer?

If neighbors have the same issue or the plumber finds the street main blocked, report it to the town.

Is the air safe to breathe?

Ventilate when safe, avoid the area and talk to a physician about any health concerns.
