What a Sudden Jump in Your Fairhaven, MA Water Bill Can Mean

A step-by-step way to tell whether a higher water bill is a leak, a billing quirk or just a change in how you use water.

☎ Call (774) 483-4896

By the Fairhaven Water Damage Pros team

A water bill that suddenly doubles is stressful, especially when nobody in the house changed their habits. In many cases the reason is a leak you cannot see, such as a running toilet, a dripping fixture or a slow line failure under a slab or in a wall.

This guide walks through simple checks you can do in an afternoon, explains what a meter can tell you and shows when it is time to call a plumber or a restoration crew.

Start with the meter

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water. Find your meter, usually in the basement or a meter pit near the street, and look at the leak indicator, often a small dial or triangle. If it moves while everything is off, water is going somewhere.

Write down the reading, wait an hour without using water and read it again. A change means a leak somewhere between the meter and the fixtures.

The toilet test

Toilets are the most common hidden leak. Drop a few drops of food coloring into the tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing and check the bowl. Color in the bowl means the flapper is leaking.

A worn flapper is a cheap repair, but it can waste a surprising amount of water over a month.

Faucets, hoses and outdoor lines

Check faucets, showerheads, the water heater area, washing machine hoses and outside spigots. A hose left connected in freezing weather can split, and a garden line can leak underground.

An irrigation system with a stuck valve can run for hours. Turn it off and see if the meter slows.

Hidden leaks and what they do

Slow leaks inside walls or under floors may not show a stain until damage is significant. Warm spots on a floor, a musty smell or a persistent hiss are clues. Slab leaks are covered on the slab leak page.

If you find wet materials, the water extraction page and the drying process explain what comes next.

Questions for the utility

Your utility can check when the meter was last read, whether the reading was estimated and whether any adjustments exist for leaks. Ask politely and bring your recent bills.

A plumber can pressure test lines and pinpoint a leak. Restoration crews dry the damage but do not repair the pipe.

Reading the bill itself

Look at the dates, the meter readings and whether any reading was estimated. An estimated reading can be too low one period and catch up the next, which looks like a sudden jump. Compare the same months in prior years, since summer watering and guests change use.

Write down your daily usage if the bill lists it. A steady high number day and night points to a leak, while spikes on a few days point to use.

Outdoor and seasonal causes

Filling a pool, running irrigation and washing cars all add up fast. A sprinkler zone that sticks open can run overnight. In winter, a faucet left dripping to protect pipes adds to the bill, but it is a fair trade against a burst pipe.

If you recently had guests, a new baby or a new appliance, use may simply have gone up. Check these before you start opening walls.

  • Irrigation timers and valves.
  • Pool or hot tub filling.
  • Dripping outdoor spigots.
  • Garden hoses left on.

When to call a plumber or a restoration crew

If the meter moves with everything off and the toilet test is clear, call a plumber to find the leak. If you already see damp walls, stained ceilings or soft floors, call the 24/7 line as well, because drying should start while the repair is arranged.

Shut off the water at the main while you wait if you can find the leak but cannot yet fix it.

Fairhaven water bills and supply

Fairhaven's drinking water comes through the Mattapoisett River Valley Water District, according to the district's 2023 water quality report. Billing questions go to the town's water department, and the Board of Public Works handles town utilities.

If you live in Acushnet or Mattapoisett, check which utility serves you, since some homes use wells.

Frequently Asked Questions

Who handles Fairhaven water billing questions?

Start with the Fairhaven Water Department or the Board of Public Works, and bring recent bills.

Can a leak raise my bill without any visible water?

Yes. Slow leaks inside walls or under slabs often show only on the bill.

Will the utility reduce my bill after a leak?

Some utilities offer adjustments. Ask the water department what applies.

How much water does a running toilet waste?

It can be many gallons a day, and it varies by leak size.

Can a leak under the house raise my bill without any water showing?

Yes. A line leaking into soil or under a slab may show nothing on the surface while the meter keeps running.
