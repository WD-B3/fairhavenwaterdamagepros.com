How to Prepare Your Fairhaven, MA Home for Storm Season

A checklist for roofs, gutters, basements and paperwork before the next coastal storm, written for Buzzards Bay homeowners.

☎ Call (774) 483-4896

By the Fairhaven Water Damage Pros team

Storm preparation is not exciting, but a few hours spent before the weather turns can save days of cleanup afterward. Fairhaven homes take the brunt of wind and tides from Buzzards Bay, and preparation starts with a short, honest look at your house.

This guide gives a checklist you can work through in a weekend, from the roof down to the basement, plus the paperwork people forget.

Roof, gutters and downspouts

Clear gutters and downspouts so water leaves the roof edge instead of backing up. Look for lifted shingles, cracked flashing and loose chimney caps. Make sure downspouts discharge away from the foundation.

Do not climb a wet roof. A roofer can check the surface safely.

Windows, doors and siding

Wind pushes rain sideways, so check caulking around windows, door thresholds and siding joints. Replace weatherstripping that no longer seals. Secure outdoor items that could become projectiles.

Basement and sump pump

Test the pump by pouring water into the pit. Clear the intake and make sure the discharge line is open. A battery backup helps in outages. Raise stored boxes and electronics off the floor.

The basement page explains what happens when water gets in anyway.

Utilities and shutoffs

Locate your main water shutoff, electrical panel and gas shutoff, and make sure everyone in the house knows where they are. Keep a flashlight, charger and key phone numbers in one place.

Know your evacuation zone. The state publishes a Fairhaven hurricane evacuation zone map.

Paperwork and photos

Take photos or video of every room, plus the roof, siding and basement. Store them online. Keep your insurance policy, a list of valuables and contact numbers where you can reach them.

Check whether your policy covers flood or wind with a separate deductible. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, per the Massachusetts Division of Insurance.

A storm kit for the house

Keep a small kit where you can reach it in the dark. Include flashlights, spare batteries, a phone charger, a battery radio, towels, a roll of plastic sheeting, duct tape and a list of key phone numbers. Add a tarp and rope if you own a boat or have exposed items.

Keep your insurance papers and photo records in a waterproof bag or saved online.

  • Flashlights and batteries.
  • Plastic sheeting and tape.
  • Towels and a bucket.
  • Phone numbers and insurance papers.

During the storm

Stay inside and away from windows. Do not go into the basement if water is rising near outlets. If power goes out, unplug sensitive electronics before it returns to avoid surges. Never use a generator indoors or in a garage.

If you see water entering, move belongings to higher spots and photograph it if you can do so safely. Call for help once the weather allows.

After the storm passes

Walk the house and the yard once it is safe. Look at the roof, windows, siding and basement. Photograph every issue. Cover openings with plastic sheeting if you can do so safely, and keep receipts for temporary repairs.

Call your insurer and the 24/7 line. The flood damage page explains what to do when water has risen from outside.

Landscaping, grading and drainage

Water that sheds from the roof has to go somewhere. Check that soil slopes away from the foundation, that downspout extensions carry water several feet from the house and that window wells are clear. Swales and gravel can guide water around a house.

Large drainage changes are a job for a contractor, and they may need permits. Small fixes like downspout extensions are cheap and make a difference in a heavy rain.

Insurance and records before the season

Read your policy before storm season and note the deductibles, especially any separate wind or hurricane deductible. Ask your agent about flood coverage, since standard policies usually exclude it. Know how to file a claim and who to call.

Store a photo inventory of each room and your valuables online. After a storm, that record is far easier than memory.

Storm history around Fairhaven

State records of 20th century hurricanes say Hurricane Bob in 1991 sent a Buzzards Bay surge of 10 to 15 feet, and Hurricane Carol in 1954 sent over 14 feet into New Bedford. A storm on December 23, 2022 flooded low areas outside the hurricane barrier, especially Goulart Memorial Drive, according to the town's vulnerability assessment.

Homeowners in Mattapoisett and Marion share the same bay.

Frequently Asked Questions

When should I prepare?

Before storm season starts, and again when a specific storm is forecast.

Do I need flood insurance?

That depends on your risk and mortgage. Check the FEMA flood map and ask your insurer or lender.

Is the Fairhaven shoreline at risk?

Parts of it are, including areas outside the New Bedford Harbor Hurricane Barrier. Check the flood map for your address.

What if a storm damages my home?

Once it is safe, photograph the damage and call the 24/7 line.

Should I sandbag my house?

Sandbags can help with low-level water, but they are not a seal. Ask the town about local guidance.
