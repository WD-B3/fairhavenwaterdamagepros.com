About Fairhaven Water Damage Pros

A Fairhaven-based water damage service that explains the work before it starts.

☎ Call (774) 483-4896

Fairhaven Water Damage Pros is a water damage restoration service based at 9 Plaza Way in Fairhaven, Massachusetts. We take emergency calls around the clock and serve Fairhaven, New Bedford, Mattapoisett, Acushnet, North Dartmouth and Marion.

This site exists to explain water damage in plain words, so that when you call you already know what to expect.

What we do

We handle the work that follows a water loss: removing standing water, finding hidden moisture, drying structures and contents, cleaning, and documenting the loss for insurance. We also explain mold remediation and sewage cleanup, which have their own safety rules.

Repairs by other trades, such as plumbing, roofing and rebuilding, are separate jobs. We tell you when you need them.

How we work

We start by asking what happened and where the water is. On site, we measure moisture before and during drying, and we record readings so that the end of the job is a number and not a feeling.

We follow the published ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage and S520 mold remediation standards as references for how the work is done. We explain scope and price before work starts, so you can decide with the facts in hand.

What you will not find here

You will not find invented reviews, made-up awards or promises about arrival times. Water damage varies too much for that. We say what we can do, explain why and let you judge.

Insurance decisions belong to your insurer, and coverage depends on your policy. We document the loss in a way you can share with them.

What a first call covers

The dispatcher asks where the water came from, whether it has stopped, how far it has spread and what materials are wet. You get safety steps right away, such as closing the main shutoff and keeping clear of electrical hazards, and a plain description of what the crew will do on arrival.

Nobody is asked to sign anything over the phone. Scope and price are explained before work starts, and you decide with the facts in hand.

Water damage and the paperwork around it

A water loss comes with a stack of paperwork: photographs, moisture logs, equipment records and, for many homeowners, an insurance claim. We keep those records because they are the proof that a house was dried properly, and because adjusters ask for them.

Questions about permits, rentals, mold, selling a house and water bills come up constantly, so the blog answers them in detail with local facts.

How this site is written

Each page explains one topic and says what a homeowner can reasonably do, what a crew does and what belongs to someone else, such as a plumber, an electrician or your insurer. Local facts come from town documents, state records and published standards, and where we could not confirm a detail we say to ask the town.

That means some answers end with 'ask the Fairhaven Building Department' or 'check the FEMA flood map for your address'. Those are the honest answers, and they are the offices that can give you the current rule for your property.

Who answers the phone

Calls go to dispatch, which is staffed around the clock. The dispatcher records your address, the source of the water and what is wet, then explains the first steps and what happens next. If a plumber, roofer or electrician is needed, dispatch says so plainly.

Nothing here replaces a qualified professional's judgment on repairs to pipes, roofs or wiring, and we say where that line is.

Safety, honestly stated

We are not doctors, engineers or lawyers, and this site does not pretend otherwise. Where a question belongs to a physician, an attorney, your insurer or the Fairhaven Building Department, we say so.

In an emergency involving fire, gas, electrical shock or a risk to life, call 911 first and the water damage line second.

Where we work

The town faces New Bedford across the Acushnet River and opens onto Buzzards Bay. Many homes are old, much of the coastline is exposed to storms and basements are common. That shapes the work, and it is why the local pages on this site discuss storm history and housing in detail.

See the service area page for the towns we serve and the services page for what we handle.
