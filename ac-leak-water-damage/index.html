AC Leak Water Damage Cleanup and Repair in Fairhaven, MA

Condensate from air conditioners and air handlers can stain ceilings and soak closets. We find the wet areas and dry them.

☎ Call (774) 483-4896

AC leak water damage in Fairhaven, MA usually starts with a clogged condensate drain line, a cracked drain pan or a failed pump on an air handler. The leak is often slow, and the first sign is a ceiling stain, a wet closet floor or a musty smell. Shut the system off at the thermostat, which stops new condensate, and call dispatch, which is open 24/7, if water is actively dripping.

This page covers water from cooling and heating equipment, including attic air handlers, closet units and ductless wall units. Roof leaks and plumbing leaks are different problems with their own pages.

Why air conditioners leak

Cooling coils pull moisture out of the air, and that water has to drain away. Algae, dust and debris clog the drain line, and when the line backs up, water spills over the drain pan or leaks from the unit. Ductless units can drip when the line is kinked or the unit is not level.

Average July temperatures in Fairhaven are about 74 degrees Fahrenheit according to the town's hazard mitigation plan, so air conditioners run through humid summer weeks, and a blocked line can drip for days before anyone notices.

Where the water ends up

Air handlers in attics drip onto ceilings. Closet units soak carpet, wall bottoms and stored items. Window and ductless units run water down the wall behind them, leaving a stain below the cassette.

Because condensate is typically clean when it leaves the coil but can carry algae, drywall and carpet that stay wet can develop odor. Prompt drying prevents most of that. The ceiling water damage page covers the attic-leak side.

What an HVAC technician fixes and what restoration fixes

An HVAC technician clears the drain line, replaces the pan or repairs the pump. Restoration crews dry the wet materials and repair the damage. Both are needed, and the order matters, since drying cannot finish while the unit still leaks.

Ask your technician to check the float switch, which shuts the system off when the pan fills.

Drying and checking for hidden moisture

Crews use a meter and thermal camera to find water in ceilings, walls and flooring. Wet insulation around ducts is removed, and drywall that stayed wet is cut out. Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the area, and readings are logged.

A musty smell after an AC leak can point to hidden moisture, and the structural drying page explains how equipment is placed.

Preventing the next leak

Have the drain line flushed each cooling season, replace filters on schedule and consider a float switch and a secondary pan under attic units. These steps are inexpensive compared with a ceiling replacement.

If the leak ran for weeks, ask about mold testing. Health questions belong to a physician, and mold remediation follows the published S520 standard.

What to do while you wait

Turn the system off at the thermostat, empty any pans carefully and catch drips with towels or a bucket. Photograph stains and the equipment label. Keep the filter and any debris from the drain line if the technician removes it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is condensate water dirty?

It is usually clean at the coil, but it can pick up algae and dust. Drying promptly matters more than the water's category.

Can I keep running the AC during repairs?

If the unit is actively leaking, turn it off at the thermostat until the drain is cleared.

How do I know the leak is from the AC?

Water that appears only during cooling and follows the equipment's path is a strong clue. A technician can confirm.

Does homeowners insurance cover an AC leak?

It depends on the policy and the cause. Sudden failures are often treated differently from gradual leaks, so ask your insurer.
