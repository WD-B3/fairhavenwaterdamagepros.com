Water Damage Restoration in Fairhaven Center, MA

Close-set historic houses and storefronts beside the Acushnet River, with water problems that start inside the walls as often as outside.

☎ Call (774) 483-4896

Fairhaven Center is the historic core of town. Town Hall, the Millicent Library and the Memorial Unitarian Church share a compact streetscape a short walk from the Acushnet River. The Massachusetts Historical Commission's survey traces the area to the Twenty Acre, a waterfront settlement that took shape by the middle of the 1700s.

This page covers what the public record says about the housing and shoreline here, and what to do when water gets into a home or storefront. Our office is at 9 Plaza Way in Fairhaven, and the line is open 24/7.

What the Center is built from

The state survey describes several building types in and around the Center. We list them because a crew reads a building by its materials and layout, not by its age.

  • Federal houses from around 1800 with sidehall plans, raised basements and stoops.
  • Narrow Greek Revival houses set close to the street.
  • Craftsman and Colonial Revival houses from the turn of the 20th century.
  • Commercial blocks such as the Phoenix Block, dated 1853 in the survey, and the Union Hotel, dated 1831.

Crews check what is actually in each building, including the supply lines, the wall cavities and the flooring. Age is not treated as proof of a problem.

The harbor side and the hurricane barrier

The town's climate vulnerability assessment says the downtown area of Fairhaven is currently protected by the New Bedford Harbor Hurricane Barrier. The same report anticipates fringe flooding along the commercial and industrial waterfront, using Union Wharf as its example.

A barrier answers storm surge, not a failed washing machine hose or a roof leak. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Look up your own address on the FEMA flood map, and read the flood damage cleanup page if tidal water is involved.

Homes and storefronts in the same blocks

Houses, offices and shops sit side by side here, so one leak can cross a party wall or run through a ceiling into a business below. Shop owners should read the commercial water damage page for how crews limit downtime.

Wet plaster and drywall need to be opened and dried, not painted over. The wall and drywall page explains how crews decide what stays.

While you wait for the crew

Follow these steps only if you can do them safely.

  1. Close the main water shutoff or the valve at the leaking fixture.
  2. Stay out of standing water if it touches outlets, cords or appliances. Do not touch the breaker panel while standing in water.
  3. Treat sewage or floodwater as contaminated and leave it to trained crews.
  4. Photograph the source and the damage before anything is moved.
  5. Call the 24/7 line and describe what you see.

Insurance and records

Your insurer decides coverage, and policies differ. According to the Massachusetts Division of Insurance, standard homeowners policies often exclude flood and sewer backup unless added. Photos, moisture readings and receipts help whatever your insurer decides, and the structural drying page explains the readings.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you work in older Fairhaven Center houses?

Yes. Crews work in houses of every period. They inspect the actual plumbing, framing and finishes rather than assuming anything from the date a house was built.

Is Fairhaven Center in a flood zone?

The town report says downtown is protected by the hurricane barrier, but flood-zone status depends on the exact address. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA flood map or ask the Fairhaven Building Department.

Can you help a shop owner near Main Street?

Yes. Call the 24/7 line and say it is a business. Crews contain the wet area, document it and explain the scope and price before work starts.

Should I open wet walls myself?

No. Cutting into wet walls can spread contamination and hit wiring or pipes. Photograph the area and let the crew take readings first.
